# Double Empathy Problem

The empathy gap between autistic and neurotypical people is symmetrical, not one-directional.

The Double Empathy Problem, originally formulated by Damian Milton (2012), proposes that the communication breakdown between autistic and non-autistic people is not caused by autistic deficits alone. Both sides struggle to understand the other. The empathy gap is symmetrical.

## Genesis

### Academic Origin: Damian Milton, 2012

Milton argued against Baron-Cohen's "empathy deficit" model of autism. The traditional view: autistic people lack theory of mind. Milton's inversion: when two people have very different experiential worlds, BOTH struggle to empathize with the other. It's a mutual breakdown, not a one-sided deficit.

## Evolution

### From Deficit Model to Mutual Mismatch

The traditional model (Baron-Cohen): Autistic people lack empathy/theory of mind. Solution: fix the autistic person.

The double empathy model (Milton): Both sides lack empathy for each other. Solution: bridge the gap from BOTH directions.

the author's extension: The AI adoption gap ([[bottleneck-economy]]) is ALSO a double empathy problem. AI doesn't understand humans. Humans don't understand AI. The solution isn't making AI more autonomous -- it's building bridges.
